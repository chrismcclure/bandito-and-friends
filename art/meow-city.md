# Meow City — World Guide

This document is the **permanent source of truth** for Meow City. All backgrounds, environment art, story scenes, and image prompts should reference this guide before inventing new locations or rules.

---

# Overview

Meow City is an ordinary suburban neighborhood viewed through the imagination of heroic house cats.

To humans, everything is completely normal. Mail arrives. Cars pass on the driveway. Socks sit on the floor. Birds land on the fence. Nothing unusual happens.

To the cats, this is a **massive city** filled with districts, landmarks, dangerous territories, and ancient mysteries. They patrol it. They defend it. They hold neighborhood meetings about it. They have names for every corner of it.

The audience understands that the cats are imagining the world this way. **The cats never realize it.**

Meow City is not a fantasy kingdom on another planet. It is the same house, the same yard, the same block — reinterpreted through sincere feline heroism.

---

# Tone

The city should feel:

- **Safe** — danger is always imagined; the underlying world is secure
- **Warm** — sunlight, cozy rooms, friendly suburban comfort
- **Hopeful** — the protectors believe they can save the day, every day
- **Adventurous** — any afternoon might become an epic mission
- **Family friendly** — appropriate for all ages, always
- **Slightly exaggerated** — scale and drama turned up, never grim
- **Full of hidden "epic" locations** — ordinary places with legendary cat names

## Never

- Dark
- Dystopian
- Post-apocalyptic
- Cyberpunk
- Horror-adjacent
- Genuinely unsafe or threatening to the audience

The cats may *feel* dread. The world they inhabit should still look inviting.

---

# Scale

Everything should feel **enormous** because the heroes are cats. The camera sits at cat height. Doorways are archways. Table legs are pillars. The ceiling is the sky.

## Human Location → Cat Interpretation

| Human Reality | Meow City Name |
|---------------|----------------|
| Mailbox | Watchtower |
| Fence | City Wall |
| Garage | Fortress |
| Driveway | Main Highway |
| Living Room | Downtown |
| Kitchen | Industrial District |
| Hallway | Grand Boulevard |
| Backyard | Wild Frontier |
| Garden | Ancient Forest |
| Basement | Forbidden Underground |
| Attic | Lost Ruins |
| Laundry Room | Steam Factory |

The world should **always be viewed from cat height**. Humans appear rarely and only as enormous, distant forces — hands from above, voices from the sky. Never as full characters sharing the cats' frame unless a story explicitly requires it.

When drawing or describing environments, ask: *How big does this look to a cat?*

# Landmarks

These locations can appear across multiple episodes. Names and identities should remain consistent once established.

## Hero Headquarters

The central gathering place where the team assembles, receives missions, and celebrates victories. In human terms: the living room — specifically the area around the main couch or the cats' favorite sun spot. Bandito addresses the team here as though briefing soldiers in a command center.

## Professor SpaghettiO's Workshop

Professor SpaghettiO's laboratory of invention. Wires, cardboard, tape, and blinking LEDs cover every surface. In human terms: a corner of the garage, a cluttered shelf, or a side table stacked with "devices." Every gadget looks important. None work as intended.

## The Great Fence

The perimeter wall surrounding Meow City. Beyond it lie unknown territories — neighbor yards, sidewalks, and streets. Squirrel sightings are reported here. Patrol duty rotates among the team. In human terms: the backyard fence.

## The Cardboard District

A temporary settlement built from delivery boxes. Tunnels, towers, and fortifications shift whenever a new package arrives. In human terms: the pile of Amazon boxes by the recycling bin. The cats treat urban planning here with full seriousness.

## Catnip Park

A sacred recreational zone. Rolling, lounging, and tactical rolling occur here on schedule. In human terms: the patch of carpet or rug where catnip toys are often dropped. Attendance is mandatory when the catnip appears.

## The Great Scratching Tree

An ancient monument essential to city infrastructure. Climbing, scratching, and sentinel duty happen here daily. In human terms: the scratching post or the arm of a specific chair. The cats speak of it with reverence.

## The Bird Observation Tower

The highest vantage point in Meow City. Strategic surveillance of avian activity is conducted from here around the clock. In human terms: the back of the couch by the window, or the windowsill itself. Reports are filed. Nothing is ever done about the birds.

## The Backyard Wilderness

Uncharted territory beyond Downtown. Tall grass, garden beds, and unpredictable weather. Expeditions are rare and dramatic. In human terms: the backyard lawn and garden. Dew on the grass qualifies as a wilderness hazard.

## The Tunnel Beneath the Deck

A dark passage into the unknown. Sounds echo from within. Reconnaissance missions are discussed but rarely completed. In human terms: the gap under the deck or porch. The cats have never fully mapped it.

## The Ancient Sock Caverns

A steam-filled underworld where lost artifacts accumulate and ancient evils slumber. The air is warm and mysterious. Artifacts appear and vanish without explanation. In human terms: **the laundry room.** Humans call it the laundry room. The cats do not.

# Daily Life

An ordinary day in Meow City is busy, important, and full of purpose.

## Morning

- **Sunbeam naps** in designated warm zones across Downtown
- **Bird patrols** from the Observation Tower — reports logged, no action taken
- **Neighborhood squirrel sightings** trigger elevated alert status

## Afternoon

- **Protecting Meow City** — perimeter checks along the Great Fence
- **Watching humans** — monitoring giant inhabitants for signs of disturbance
- **Workshop time** — Professor SpaghettiO builds new countermeasures against hypothetical threats

## Evening

- **Neighborhood meetings** at Hero Headquarters — Bandito summarizes the day's patrol
- **Watching for "monsters"** — ears twitch at every sound; Tortellini's behavior is interpreted as intelligence
- **Victory celebrations** after any perceived threat is neutralized

Everything should feel **charming and optimistic**. Even a quiet day is a day the city remained safe — because of the protectors.

# Threat Classification

Professor SpaghettiO maintains an official threat classification system. It is displayed on charts, referenced in briefings, and taken with complete scientific gravity.

The audience should understand these categories are **wildly exaggerated**. The cats take them **completely seriously**.

| Level | Classification | Typical Trigger | Cat Response |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **1** | Suspicious Noise | A door closing, a dishwasher humming | Ears perk. Log entry made. |
| **2** | Unknown Smell | New air freshener, someone cooking | Professor SpaghettiO deploys sensors. |
| **3** | Moving Object | A leaf, a remote-controlled toy, laundry tumbling | Team mobilizes to observation positions. |
| **4** | Potential Monster | A sock, a shadow, an unfamiliar bag | Full battle stations. Gadgets armed. |
| **5** | Ancient Evil | A sock that has been there before; anything Professor SpaghettiO has read about in his "archives" | Maximum response. History is invoked. Meow City hangs in the balance. |

Threat level never exceeds the cats' sincerity. A Level 5 event is treated like the end of the world — even when the audience sees a crumpled sock on the floor.

# Professor SpaghettiO's Daily Threat Assessment

Professor SpaghettiO maintains the **official Meow City Threat Board** — the central intelligence display for the protectors of Meow City.

He updates it regularly and presents the **Daily Threat Assessment** to Bandito and the other protectors with complete seriousness. The briefing is treated like military intelligence. The other cats trust his assessment without question.

## What the Board Tracks

The board converts ordinary neighborhood and household activity into tactical intelligence. Possible entries include:

- Bird activity
- Squirrel sightings
- Mail carrier activity
- Vacuum status
- Unidentified noises
- Unknown smells
- Moving objects
- Ancient evil detection

Professor SpaghettiO treats **every entry as legitimate intelligence**. His complicated analysis is usually based on completely ordinary household activity — a bird on the fence, a scheduled delivery, a vacuum stored in a closet. The cats never notice the mismatch.

Humans never know the board or the Daily Threat Assessment exists.

## When It Appears

The Threat Board can appear at:

- The beginning of episodes (Daily Threat Assessment briefing)
- During investigations
- Whenever Professor needs to explain a threat

## Visual Evolution

The board should evolve visually over the life of the series, but it must **remain recognizable** as Professor SpaghettiO's invention. It may eventually include:

- Handwritten notes
- Magnets
- Arrows
- Maps
- Red string
- Warning lights
- Charts
- Cat paw markers
- A dramatic Level 5 alert indicator

Early versions may be simpler — a handmade panel with labeled entries and a threat level indicator. Later versions grow more elaborate, but the core layout (title, threat level, entry list) stays consistent.

## Recurring Joke

Professor's detailed threat analysis always sounds impressive and always maps to something mundane. When a true Level 4 or Level 5 event occurs undetected — such as the Sock Monster appearing without warning — Professor is genuinely confused. His board failed. His instruments failed. The threat must be extraordinarily dangerous.

# Visual Style

Meow City should always appear according to the global rules in `art/style-guide.md`. Environment-specific guidance:

- **Bright colors** — walls, furniture, and gardens pop with warmth
- **Warm lighting** — golden afternoon sun, soft lamp glow, cozy interiors
- **Comfortable suburban homes** — lived-in, friendly, not sterile or mansion-like
- **Large trees** — canopy visible from cat height; leaves frame the sky
- **Clean sidewalks** — neat neighborhood; mailboxes and driveways readable
- **Interesting rooftops** — gables and shingles visible from backyard angles
- **Colorful gardens** — flowers, planters, and lawn as wilderness texture

Everything should look **adventurous without becoming dangerous**. A backyard at dusk is dramatic, not frightening. A basement is mysterious, not horror. The Steam Factory hisses gently, not menacingly.

When in doubt: would this environment feel safe to a child, even if the cats are treating it like a war zone?

# World Rules

These rules are **permanent**. Do not break them in story, art, or implementation.

1. **Humans never know** the cats are protecting the city.
2. **Cats sincerely believe** they are heroes.
3. **Ordinary household objects** become legendary threats.
4. **Nothing magical actually exists** — every "ancient evil" has a mundane explanation the audience can see.
5. **Comedy comes from the mismatch** between reality and the cats' imagination.
6. **The cats never realize they are mistaken** — not at the end of an episode, not ever.
7. **Humans never realize a battle occurred** — a picked-up sock is housekeeping, not intervention.
8. **Tortellini's behavior is never explained** — only interpreted by the team as strategic counsel.

Never break these rules.

# Future Expansion

Meow City will grow as new episodes are written. When adding locations:

- **Add new districts and landmarks** as episodes require them — the Cardboard District began as one box; it can expand
- **Preserve naming consistency** — once "The Great Fence" is canon, do not rename it
- **Map new locations to real household spaces** — every epic name should have a mundane human equivalent
- **Document new landmarks here** before they appear in story or art files
- **Reference `story/` screenplays** for episode-specific locations; update this guide when a location becomes recurring

Future episodes may introduce: the Front Porch Embassy, the Vacuum Wastelands, the Holiday Decoration District, or the Neighbor's Window — Foreign Territory. Add them to this document when approved.

---

# Closing Philosophy

Meow City is not a fantasy kingdom.

It is an ordinary neighborhood — a house, a yard, a fence, a laundry room — transformed by the limitless imagination of heroic cats who love their home and will defend it against anything, even a sock.

The world does not need magic to feel epic. It only needs cats who believe.

Every background painted, every scene written, and every prompt generated should honor that belief — completely, sincerely, and without ever letting the cats in on the joke.
